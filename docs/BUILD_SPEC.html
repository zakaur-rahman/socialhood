Rules for the coding agent

  • MUST, SHOULD and MAY carry their RFC 2119 meanings. A MUST that cannot be met blocks the task.
  • Release tags mark scope: R1 is the first launch, R2 the next release, R3 later. Do not build R2 or R3 items during R1 tasks, but do not paint R1 into a corner that blocks them.
  • If two documents disagree, the document named in the last column of the table above wins for its topic. Record the conflict in docs/CONFLICTS.md so the owner can fix the source.
  • If something needed is not specified, do not invent product behaviour. Add it to docs/QUESTIONS.md, pick the most conservative option (the one that sends nothing to customers and exposes nothing across workspaces), and mark it in code with # SPEC-GAP: <question id>.
  • Names in this spec (tables, columns, enum values, routes, event names, env vars) are exact. Use them verbatim.
  • Platform API details (Meta Graph, WhatsApp Cloud, Dodo) were current when written. Where a line says verify at build time, check the provider's current documentation before coding it and note the result in the PR.

Product requirements

What Social Hood v2 does, who it is for, what ships in the first release, and how we will know it worked. It builds on the earlier PRD's vision and narrows it to a first release that can be built, measured and sold.

1.1Summary and problem

Social Hood is an inbox and automation tool for businesses that sell and support customers through Instagram and WhatsApp. It puts every conversation in one place, answers repetitive messages automatically, and uses AI to understand what customers want and draft replies grounded in the business's own information.

The people it serves run small businesses and creator brands. Their customers ask the same questions in DMs and comments all day: price, availability, shipping, "link please". They lose sales when replies are slow, and they lose trust when automated replies are wrong. They do not have time to read hundreds of comments to learn what their audience thinks.

v1, built as a school project and never deployed, established the shape of the product: a unified inbox, keyword automations, an AI auto-bot and post scheduling all exist in its code. Reading it also showed what breaks: prompts that never reached the model, replies sent twice, messages reported as sent when they failed, and data readable across accounts. v2 is the commercial product. It keeps what works in v1 (the inbox design in particular), fixes those failures structurally, and adds the intelligence layer from the vision: understanding, grounded suggestions, safe automatic replies, and comment insight.

Product loopObserve (messages, comments, posts) → Understand (intent, sentiment, lead signal) → Decide (answer, suggest, or escalate) → Act (reply, DM, publish) → Measure (response time, acceptance, sentiment) → Learn (knowledge gaps, content that works). R1 closes the loop for conversations and comments. Content learning arrives in R2.

Glossary

Workspace
The business account in Social Hood. Owns connected accounts, conversations, automations, knowledge, billing. A user can belong to several (R2).
Connected account
One Instagram professional account or one WhatsApp business phone number linked to a workspace. Stored as social_accounts.
Contact
A customer on a platform (an Instagram user or a WhatsApp number) who has messaged or commented.
Conversation
The thread between one connected account and one contact.
Reply window
The period during which the platform allows a free-form reply: 24 hours from the contact's last message on Instagram and WhatsApp, extendable to 7 days on Instagram for human replies with the Human Agent feature.
Analysis
The AI's reading of an inbound message: intent, sentiment, priority, lead score, topics, whether a human is needed.
Suggested reply
An AI-drafted reply the user can send, edit, regenerate or dismiss.
AI mode
Per connected account (overridable per conversation): Off, Suggest (drafts only) or Auto (sends when every safety check passes).
Automation
A user-authored rule: when a DM or comment contains a keyword, send a fixed message or an AI reply guided by instructions.
Knowledge
Business information the AI may use: FAQs, policies, product details, web pages, documents.
AI credit
The unit that meters AI usage against the plan allowance.

1.2Goals and success metrics

R1 goals

  1. Parity without the defects. Everything that works in v1 works in v2, and none of the defects in §0.3 can recur.
  2. Useful understanding. Every inbound message and comment is tagged accurately enough that users trust the tags to sort their day.
  3. Replies users actually send. Suggested replies are good enough that most are sent with little or no editing.
  4. Safe automation. Automatic replies never go out when a human is needed, never break platform rules, and never repeat.
  5. A business that charges correctly. Paid features require a paid plan confirmed by the payment provider.

1.3Users

Primary: owner-operator

Runs a small business or creator brand, sells through Instagram first and WhatsApp second. One to three accounts, 20 to 500 DMs a day, many comments on launches. Answers on a phone between other work. Often in India, selling in INR, writing in English, Hindi or Hinglish.

  • Wants to reply fast to buyers without typing the same answer fifty times.
  • Wants to know which conversations are real leads or angry customers.
  • Is afraid of a bot saying something wrong in their name.

Secondary: in-house social manager

Manages a brand's accounts full time at a desk. Cares about response time, the backlog, what the audience says under posts, and scheduling.

  • Wants triage: what needs a human now.
  • Wants a summary of the reaction to a post without scrolling through it.
  • Wants teammates in the same inbox (R2).

Agencies managing many clients are served in R2 (multiple workspaces per user, members, roles).

Jobs to be done

  • When a buyer asks a routine question, answer correctly within minutes, even while I'm busy.
  • When my inbox is full, show me the conversations that need me first.
  • When a post gets hundreds of comments, tell me what people are saying and let me act on the ones that matter.
  • When someone comments a keyword, DM them the link or offer automatically.
  • When I plan content, schedule it once and trust it to go out.

1.6Functional requirements

Each requirement lists its priority, release and acceptance criteria. Acceptance criteria are the minimum test cases for the task that builds the requirement.

Account and workspace

FR-ACC-01
Sign up and sign in MUST R1

Email with password and Google sign-in through Clerk. One sign-in page and one sign-up page.

  • A new user reaches onboarding within 3 s of completing sign-up.
  • A returning user lands on the inbox of the workspace they used last.
  • A signed-out visit to any app route redirects to sign-in and back afterwards.
FR-ACC-02
Personal workspace on first sign-in MUST R1

Create a workspace named "{first name}'s workspace" (or "My workspace" if no name), with a unique URL slug, a Free subscription and the user as owner.

  • Two concurrent first requests create exactly one workspace.
  • A user whose Clerk email is unverified is never linked to an existing account by email.
FR-ACC-03
Workspace settings MUST R1

Name, slug (changeable, unique), timezone (IANA name, defaulted from the browser), default reply language.

  • All scheduling screens show and accept times in the workspace timezone.
FR-ACC-04
Onboarding checklist MUST R1

Four steps on Home until dismissed: connect an account, add knowledge, choose an AI mode, create an automation. Each step links to its screen and ticks itself when done.

  • Progress is computed from data, not from clicks.
FR-ACC-05
Delete workspace MUST R1

Owner deletes the workspace by typing its name. Platform tokens are destroyed immediately, webhooks for its accounts are ignored from then on, and all rows are purged within 24 h. An active paid subscription is cancelled first.

FR-ACC-06
Members and roles SHOULD R2

Invite by email; roles owner, admin, agent. The R1 schema and authorization checks already carry roles, with every R1 user an owner.

Connections

FR-CON-01
Connect Instagram MUST R1

Connect an Instagram professional (business or creator) account through Instagram Login. On success: store the account, subscribe it to webhooks, sync its 25 most recent posts and their comments, and backfill recent conversations where the API allows.

  • The account appears with username and picture within 5 s of returning from Instagram.
  • A personal (non-professional) account is rejected with the message in §4.7.
  • Connecting an account already connected to another workspace is refused and says which is required: disconnect there first.
FR-CON-02
Connect WhatsApp MUST R1

Connect a WhatsApp Business number through Meta's Embedded Signup (v4; v2 is deprecated on 15 October 2026). The user picks the business, WhatsApp Business Account and number inside Meta's dialog; the app never picks "the first one".

  • The number shows its display name and phone number after connecting.
  • The app is subscribed to the WhatsApp Business Account's webhooks.
  • If Meta refuses because Social Hood's onboarding allowance is used up (10 new businesses per rolling 7 days until verification, 200 after), the user sees a friendly "connections are paused" message and the attempt is logged for follow-up.
FR-CON-03
Several accounts per workspace MUST R1

Up to the plan limit per platform. Each account has its own AI mode and automations, and the inbox filters by account.

FR-CON-04
Account health MUST R1

Status active, needs_reconnect, disconnected or error, with the last error in plain language. An account that needs reconnecting shows a banner in the inbox and a notification.

FR-CON-05
Automatic token refresh MUST R1

Instagram long-lived tokens are refreshed when fewer than 20 days remain. A failed refresh sets needs_reconnect and notifies the owner by email.

FR-CON-06
Disconnect MUST R1

Deletes the stored token at once and stops processing the account's webhooks. The user chooses whether to keep or delete the account's conversations and comments.

FR-CON-07
Facebook Pages SHOULD R2

Messenger, comments and publishing for Pages.

Inbox

FR-INB-01
Unified conversation list MUST R1

All conversations across connected accounts, newest activity first. Filters: platform or account; All, Unread, Needs reply, Leads, AI handled; Archived. Search by contact name, username and message text.

  • Filters are applied by the server with cursor pagination; a busy Instagram account never hides WhatsApp conversations.
  • Search returns within 500 ms at 100k messages per workspace.
FR-INB-02
Conversation view MUST R1

Full history with older messages loaded on scroll, date separators, and every attachment type rendered: image, video, audio, file, story mention, story reply, shared post, sticker, unsupported (with a "View in Instagram" link).

  • Switching conversations never shows the previous conversation's drafts, failed messages or scroll position.
FR-INB-03
Real-time updates MUST R1

New messages, status changes, analyses and suggestions appear without reloading, in every open tab.

  • p95 ≤ 3 s from webhook receipt to display.
  • After a network drop, missed events are replayed on reconnect.
FR-INB-04
Read and unread MUST R1

Opening a conversation marks it read; "Mark unread" reverses it. The Inbox nav item shows the number of unread conversations.

FR-INB-05
Archive MUST R1

Archive and unarchive. An archived conversation returns to the inbox when the contact writes again.

FR-INB-06
Reply MUST R1

Text reply with Enter to send and Shift+Enter for a new line. Drafts are kept per conversation for the session.

FR-INB-07
Honest delivery status MUST R1

Every outbound message shows Sending, Sent, Delivered or Read where the platform reports them, or Failed with a readable reason and Retry. The API never reports success for a send the platform rejected.

  • A platform error on send produces a Failed bubble with the mapped reason within 5 s.
  • Retry resends the same message once; the platform never receives it twice for one click.
FR-INB-08
Attachments MUST R1

Send images, video, audio and files on both platforms, within each platform's limits: Instagram images up to 8 MB, video, audio (AAC, M4A, WAV) and PDF files up to 25 MB; WhatsApp images up to 5 MB, video and audio up to 16 MB, and PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint or text documents up to 100 MB. Stickers: Instagram's heart; WhatsApp 512 × 512 WebP stickers up to 500 KB. Received files, audio, video and stickers are shown in the thread. Inbound media is copied to our storage because platform media URLs expire.

FR-INB-09
Replies from the native app MUST R1

Messages the business sends from the Instagram app appear as outbound, labelled "Sent from Instagram".

FR-INB-10
Reply-window rules MUST R1

The composer always shows the window state. Instagram: open for 24 h after the contact's last message; then, if Human Agent is enabled, human replies are allowed for 7 days with the tag; then closed. WhatsApp: open for 24 h; then only an approved template can be sent, chosen from a template picker.

  • The server rejects a send outside the window with error code reply_window_closed, even if the UI allowed it.
  • AI auto replies and automations never use the Human Agent tag.
FR-INB-11
Contact panel MUST R1

Name, username, platform, picture, first seen, number of conversations, lead score, latest analysis and summary.

FR-INB-12

FR-INB-14
Follow-up reminders SHOULD R1

A lead can go quiet after the business replies, and once 24 hours pass since the customer's last message the business can no longer write to them. Remind the user before that happens: an open conversation with lead score ≥ 60, whose customer last wrote 18–22 hours ago, gets a "Closing soon" signal in the list, appears under a "Closing soon" view, and triggers one notification ("Follow up with Priya: the reply window closes in 3 h") with a "Schedule follow-up" shortcut.

  • One reminder per conversation per window; a new customer message starts a new window.
  • No reminder for archived conversations or when the business has already sent a message after the customer's last one within the last 2 hours.

  • A burst of five messages in 20 s produces one analysis of the latest message, with the burst as context.
FR-AI-02

FR-SUG-01

FR-SUG-02

  • Ready within 12 s p95 of the message.
  • Edit puts the text in the composer; sending it records "edited" and the edit distance.
  • A new inbound message replaces an unused suggestion.
FR-SUG-03

If the answer needs facts not in knowledge (price, stock, policy, dates), the suggestion is replaced by a note to the user: "Not in your knowledge: shipping to Dubai", with a link to add it. The AI never states a price, policy or availability that is not in knowledge.

FR-SUG-04

  • Every decision (sent, suggested, escalated, skipped) is recorded with its reasons and shown in the thread.
FR-SUG-05

FR-SUG-07

Limit Auto to chosen hours.

Automations

FR-AUT-01
Create an automation MUST R1

Fields: name; connected Instagram account; trigger (a DM contains a keyword, a comment contains a keyword, or any comment on selected posts); keywords (1–50; none for the any-comment trigger); match mode (whole word by default, exact message, or contains); action (send a message built in the DM builder, FR-AUT-13, or an AI reply guided by instructions); for comment triggers, public reply variations (FR-AUT-14) and the post scope (all posts, selected posts, or the next post published, FR-AUT-18); settings (cooldown, run window, order when busy, disclosure).

FR-AUT-02
Validation before activation MUST R1

Activation needs an account, a trigger, at least one keyword (the any-comment trigger instead needs selected posts or the next post) and a non-empty action. AI replies need a paid plan. The error lists every missing field.

FR-AUT-03
List and status MUST R1

Active or paused, trigger, keywords, and runs in the last 7 days. Pause and resume from the list.

FR-AUT-04
Run log MUST R1

Every firing: time, contact, the message or comment, what was sent, result and error.

FR-AUT-05
Cooldown MUST R1

An automation fires at most once per contact per 24 h (configurable 0–72 h). Comment triggers additionally fire at most once per comment.

FR-AUT-06
Matching rules MUST R1

Text and keywords are normalised (Unicode NFKC, case-folded, whitespace collapsed). Whole word: the keyword appears with word boundaries. Exact: the whole message equals a keyword. Contains: substring. Only active automations of the account that received the event are considered; the lowest priority number wins, then the oldest.

  • "price" matches "What's the PRICE?" but not "priceless" in whole-word mode.
  • A keyword in workspace A never matches an event on workspace B's account.
  • A paused automation never fires.
FR-AUT-07
Precedence over AI MUST R1

If an automation fires for a DM, no AI suggestion or auto reply is made for that message.

FR-AUT-08
Comment actions MUST R1

Send the private reply (DM) to the commenter and, if set, the public reply. A failed private reply does not stop the public reply.

FR-AUT-09
Story triggers and sequences SHOULD R2

Story mention and story reply triggers (R2); multi-step follow-ups (R3); an assistant that builds automations from chat (R3).

FR-AUT-10
Viral comment surges MUST R1

Instagram allows 750 private replies per hour per account, and one private reply per comment within 7 days. When a comment automation matches faster than that, matches wait in a per-account queue instead of failing. The automation page shows the queue ("2,140 DMs waiting · all sent in about 3 h") and offers three modes: oldest first (default), newest first (reach people who just commented), and public reply only (skip the DM and post only the public reply, which counts against the account's general API budget, not the 750/hour cap). The user can also pause. A match still waiting when its comment turns 7 days old is skipped with the reason "Instagram's 7-day limit passed".

  • With 100,000 matches in an hour, the account never exceeds 750 private replies in any hour, and none is attempted after 7 days.
  • The ETA updates as the queue drains.
  • DMs, sends and other accounts in the same workspace are not delayed.
FR-AUT-11
Automation disclosure SHOULD R1

A workspace setting appends a short line (default "Sent automatically") to messages sent by automations and AI auto replies. It is on by default for workspaces in places whose law requires bots to identify themselves (the list is reviewed by a lawyer before launch) and optional elsewhere. The line counts toward the platform's text limit.

FR-AUT-12
Start from a template MUST R1

New automation opens a gallery of ready-made automations that fill in the trigger, keywords, messages and settings, which the user then edits. R1 set: Send a link to commenters (comment keyword → DM with a link button); Giveaway replies (any comment on a chosen post → public reply variations and a DM with the rules); Price on request (comment "price" → AI reply from knowledge); Catalogue by DM (DM keyword → link button); Answer FAQs (DM keywords such as "shipping" → AI reply); Book a call (DM "book" → booking link button). "Start from blank" is always offered. Templates are defined in code, and the template used is stored on the automation.

FR-AUT-13
DM builder MUST R1

Automation messages combine text with personal fields ({first_name}, {username}, each with a fallback such as "there"), one optional image, and up to 3 link buttons (title up to 20 characters, https URL). The editor shows a live Instagram-style preview and a byte counter (TR-PL-10). Buttons use Instagram's button template; T0.9 confirms its limits.

  • A contact without a known first name gets the fallback, never the literal {first_name}.
  • A non-https or malformed URL blocks activation with the field highlighted.
FR-AUT-14
Public reply variations SHOULD R1

Up to 5 public reply texts per comment automation, chosen at random so replies on a busy post don't look copied and pasted. The same text is never used twice in a row on one post. Personal fields work here too.

FR-AUT-15
Overlaps and priority MUST R1

When a keyword (after normalisation) is also used by another active automation of the same account and trigger type, the editor names that automation and says which one runs first. Only the first matching automation runs for a message or comment; keyword automations run before any-comment ones. The user sets the order by dragging automations in the list, per account, with a keyboard alternative (Move up, Move down).

FR-AUT-16
Results per automation MUST R1

Each automation shows, for the last 7 or 30 days: runs, DMs sent, public replies, people who replied within 24 h of the DM, skipped (with reasons: cooldown, 7-day limit, outside run window), failures, and how many are queued now, with a daily chart. The list shows 7-day runs with a small trend line and the time of the last run.

FR-AUT-17
Run window SHOULD R1

Optional start and end time, for launches and giveaways. Before the start the automation shows as Scheduled; at the end it pauses itself, shows as Ended and notifies its creator.

FR-AUT-18
Automations for upcoming posts SHOULD R1

A comment automation can target "the next post I publish" (from Social Hood or the Instagram app) or a specific scheduled post. In the post composer, "Add comment automation" creates one for that post from a template. It links itself to the post the moment it is published, so the first comments are answered.

FR-AUT-19
Find and manage MUST R1

The list can be searched and filtered by account, status and trigger, sorted by recent runs, name or creation date, and grouped by account. Row menu: Duplicate, Pause or Resume, Delete. Several automations can be paused at once.

FR-AUT-20
Link click tracking SHOULD R2

Link buttons can go through a Social Hood redirect that counts clicks per automation and button (count and time only), shown in the automation's results.

FR-AUT-21
Tap first SHOULD R1

A comment automation can open with a short private reply (default "Hi {first_name|there}! Tap the button below, or just reply here, and I'll send it right over 👇") carrying one quick-reply button (default "Send me the link", up to 20 characters). The automation's message (text, image, link buttons) is sent when the commenter taps the button or replies with anything, since quick replies are not shown on desktop. Their answer opens Instagram's 24-hour window and allows the profile lookup, so the message uses their real first name and can include an image and buttons, which private replies cannot carry. The answering message is marked automation_handled and no DM keyword automation runs on it. The run shows "Waiting for tap" until then; results count taps. On by default for new comment automations.

FR-AUT-22
Follow nudge, never a follow gate SHOULD R1

An automation can send one more message after its message, only to people Instagram reports as not following the account (is_user_follow_business; skipped when unknown), for example "Enjoying this? Follow us for more like it." with a "View profile" link button. The message itself is never held back or conditioned on following or sharing: Meta's Spam Community Standard forbids "requiring users to engage (in the form of likes, shares, follows, or any other public-facing form of engagement) to gain access to specific, exclusive content", and shares cannot be seen through the API. The editor says so. Results count nudges; runs record whether the person followed when the message went out; the inbox shows "Follows you" when known.

FR-CMT-07

2.4API conventions

TR-API-01
Paths and versioning

Every product endpoint is under /v1. Workspace data is always addressed through the path: /v1/w/{workspace_id}/…. Public webhook endpoints live under /webhooks/… and are unversioned. JSON field names are snake_case.

TR-API-02
IDs and time

All ids are UUIDs (v4, generated by Postgres). Timestamps are ISO 8601 in UTC with Z. The client converts to the workspace timezone for display.

TR-API-03
Errors

Every non-2xx response uses application/problem+json:

{ "type": "https://api.socialhood.com/errors/reply_window_closed",
  "title": "The reply window has closed",
  "status": 409,
  "code": "reply_window_closed",
  "detail": "Instagram only allows replies within 24 hours of the customer's last message.",
  "errors": [ { "field": "text", "message": "..." } ],   // validation only
  "request_id": "01J..." }

Codes: validation_error 422, unauthorized 401, forbidden 403, not_found 404, conflict 409, idempotency_conflict 409, reply_window_closed 409, account_needs_reconnect 409, capability_unavailable 409, entitlement_required 402, quota_exceeded 402, unsupported_media 415, rate_limited 429, platform_error 502, internal 500. A resource in another workspace returns 404, never 403.

TR-API-04
Pagination

Cursor pagination on every list: query cursor and limit (default 30, max 100); response { "items": [...], "next_cursor": "..." | null }. The cursor is an opaque base64url of the sort key and id (for example (last_message_at, id)), so ties never skip rows.

TR-API-05
Idempotency

POST endpoints that send or schedule anything to a customer or platform require an Idempotency-Key header (UUID from the client). The key, a hash of the body and the response are stored for 24 h in idempotency_keys. Same key and body: return the stored response. Same key, different body: 409 idempotency_conflict.

TR-API-06
Asynchronous actions

Actions completed by the worker return 202 Accepted with the resource in its pending state (for example a message with status: "queued"). Progress arrives over SSE. No endpoint returns success for work that has not happened.

TR-API-07
Rate limits

Redis sliding windows, returning 429 with Retry-After: 300 requests/min per user overall; 60 sends/min per workspace; 20 AI requests/min per workspace (regenerate, caption, knowledge test); 30/min per IP on public OAuth callbacks. Webhook endpoints are not rate-limited by IP (Meta shares IPs) but cap bodies at 5 MB (TR-WH-01).

TR-API-08
OpenAPI is the contract

FastAPI's generated OpenAPI is committed as apps/api/openapi.json and regenerated by a script. CI fails if the committed file differs from the generated one, or if packages/api-client is stale.

2.5Auth and tenancy

Authentication

TR-AUTH-01
Clerk session JWT on every request

The web app sends Authorization: Bearer <token> from Clerk's getToken() on every API call. Tokens live about 60 s; Clerk refreshes them, so the client asks for a token per request rather than caching one. Cookies are never used for API auth.

TR-AUTH-02
Local verification, no per-request Clerk call

The API verifies the JWT with the instance public key (CLERK_JWT_KEY, PEM). No network call is made per request.

# auth/clerk.py
import jwt

def verify_session_token(token: str) -> str:
    try:
        claims = jwt.decode(
            token, settings.clerk_jwt_key, algorithms=["RS256"],
            issuer=settings.clerk_issuer, leeway=5,
            options={"require": ["exp", "iat", "nbf", "sub", "iss"]},
        )
    except jwt.PyJWTError as e:
        raise Unauthorized("invalid_token") from e
    azp = claims.get("azp")
    if azp is not None and azp not in settings.clerk_authorized_parties:
        raise Unauthorized("invalid_azp")
    return claims["sub"]   # Clerk user id
TR-AUTH-03
Provisioning

If no users row has the Clerk id, fetch the user once from GET https://api.clerk.com/v1/users/{id}, then in one transaction insert the user, a workspace, the owner membership and a Free subscription. Use INSERT … ON CONFLICT (clerk_user_id) DO NOTHING and re-read, so concurrent first requests create one user. Link to an existing row by email only if that email's Clerk verification.status is verified.

TR-AUTH-04
Clerk webhook

POST /webhooks/clerk, verified with svix over the raw body. user.updated updates name, email and avatar. user.deleted deletes workspaces the user solely owns (FR-ACC-05) and the user row.

TR-AUTH-05
Last seen

users.last_seen_at is written at most once per 15 minutes per user (checked against a Redis key), never on every request.

Tenancy

Three layers, so a missing check in one place cannot expose data.

TR-TEN-01
Layer 1: the route dependency

Every /v1/w/{workspace_id}/… route depends on workspace_ctx, which loads the membership for (workspace_id, current user) and returns 404 if there is none. It sets the context variable current_workspace_id for the rest of the request and exposes the member's role.

TR-TEN-02
Layer 2: automatic filtering in the ORM

Every tenant table inherits TenantScoped. A session event adds workspace_id = current to every ORM SELECT touching those tables, and raises if no workspace is set. Updates and deletes go only through repository helpers that add the filter explicitly.

# db/tenancy.py
current_workspace_id: ContextVar[uuid.UUID | None] = ContextVar("ws", default=None)
tenant_bypass: ContextVar[bool] = ContextVar("tenant_bypass", default=False)

class TenantScoped:
    workspace_id: Mapped[uuid.UUID] = mapped_column(
        ForeignKey("workspaces.id", ondelete="CASCADE"), index=True)

@event.listens_for(Session, "do_orm_execute")
def _tenant_filter(state: ORMExecuteState) -> None:
    if (not state.is_select or state.is_column_load
            or state.is_relationship_load or tenant_bypass.get()):
        return
    # all_mappers alone misses select(func.count()).select_from(Model), which then runs
    # unfiltered; also scan every table the statement uses (sqlalchemy.sql.util.find_tables).
    if not touches_tenant_table(state):
        return
    ws = current_workspace_id.get()
    if ws is None:
        raise TenancyError("tenant query without workspace context")
    state.statement = state.statement.options(
        with_loader_criteria(TenantScoped, lambda cls: cls.workspace_id == ws,
                             include_aliases=True))

# repositories/base.py: the only sanctioned way to update or delete tenant rows
def scoped_update(model, **where):
    return update(model).where(model.workspace_id == _require_ws(), *_eq(model, where))
def scoped_delete(model, **where):
    return delete(model).where(model.workspace_id == _require_ws(), *_eq(model, where))

Webhook processing and jobs that start without a workspace (for example "which account owns this Instagram id?") use with tenant_bypass_scope(): for that single lookup only, then set current_workspace_id from the result before doing anything else.

TR-TEN-03
Layer 3: the isolation test suite

tests/tenancy/test_isolation.py seeds workspaces A and B with one of every resource. It walks the OpenAPI document and, for every route with an id in its path, calls it as a member of A with B's ids. Every call must return 404 and change nothing. A new route without coverage fails CI.

TR-TEN-04
Lint rule

A CI check fails if update( or delete( from SQLAlchemy is used outside repositories/, or if tenant_bypass_scope appears outside webhooks/, jobs/ and auth/.

TR-TEN-05
Roles

owner can do everything, including billing and deleting the workspace. admin can do everything except billing and deletion. agent can use the inbox and comments only. R1 users are all owners, but routes declare their minimum role now with require_role("admin").

TR-TEN-06
Layer 4 (R2): row-level security

In R2, enable Postgres row-level security on every tenant table as a fourth layer: the API sets SET LOCAL app.workspace_id at the start of each transaction, policies compare workspace_id to it, and the application's database role is not the table owner (or tables use FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY). Webhook routing and migrations use a separate role that bypasses the policies.

2.6Webhook ingestion

Webhooks are the product's main input. They must be verified, stored once, acknowledged fast, and processed exactly once from the customer's point of view.

TR-WH-01
Raw body first

Webhook routes read await request.body() before any parsing. Signature checks run on those exact bytes. There is no global JSON body parser in FastAPI, so this is the default; do not add middleware that consumes the body. Webhook routes accept bodies up to 5 MB (WhatsApp payloads can reach 3 MB); larger bodies get 413 and are logged. Other routes keep SEC-08's 1 MB limit.

TR-WH-02
Verify or reject, always

Missing configuration is a startup error, not a reason to skip verification. An invalid or missing signature returns 401 and stores nothing.

def reply_window(conv, platform, now, human_agent_enabled) -> ReplyWindow:
    if conv.last_inbound_at is None:
        return ReplyWindow(state="closed", closes_at=None)
    open_until = conv.last_inbound_at + timedelta(hours=24)
    if now < open_until:
        return ReplyWindow(state="open", closes_at=open_until)
    if platform == "instagram" and human_agent_enabled:
        ha_until = conv.last_inbound_at + timedelta(days=7)
        if now < ha_until:
            return ReplyWindow(state="human_agent", closes_at=ha_until)
    if platform == "whatsapp":
        return ReplyWindow(state="template_only", closes_at=None)
    return ReplyWindow(state="closed", closes_at=None)

Platform limits

TR-PL-09 Per-account token buckets in Valkey guard every platform call, sized to Meta's published limits: Instagram Send API 100 calls/s for text, links, reactions and stickers, and 10/s for audio and video (images use the 10/s bucket until T0.9 confirms otherwise); private replies 750/hour; Conversations API 2/s; WhatsApp 80 messages/s per number and 1 message every 6 s to the same user. A job that would exceed a bucket re-schedules itself for when a token is free, which does not count as a retry. Instagram's other calls share a rolling 24 h budget of 4,800 × the account's impressions: when a response carries X-Business-Use-Case-Usage above 80%, polling for that account pauses, and on error 80002 all calls for it stop until estimated_time_to_regain_access. All workers share the buckets, so adding workers never raises the send rate.

TR-PL-10 Outbound text limits are enforced in the composer, the API and AI output: Instagram 1,000 bytes of UTF-8 (about 330 Devanagari characters or 250 emoji), WhatsApp 4,096 characters. For Instagram the composer counts bytes and blocks sending past the limit; the API returns 422 validation_error on text; AI replies are generated under the limit and trimmed at a sentence boundary if needed. The disclosure line (FR-AUT-11) counts toward the limit.

Message analysis

TR-AI-05
Pipeline and schema
  1. Inbound customer message inserted → enqueue analyze_conversation(conversation_id) with queueing lock analyze:{conversation_id}, deferred 4 s. A second message during the delay does not enqueue again, which batches bursts.
  2. The job skips if AI analysis is off for the account or quota is exhausted (records a skipped decision).
  3. It loads the last 12 messages, marks the newest inbound one as TARGET, reserves 1 credit, and calls generate_json with AI_MODEL_ANALYSIS, temperature 0, max 400 tokens, timeout 8 s.
  4. It stores message_analyses, updates the conversation's cached signals (needs_human, lead_score, priority, last_intent, last_sentiment), publishes analysis.created and conversation.updated.
  5. If needs_reply and the effective AI mode is Suggest or Auto, it enqueues suggest_reply.
  6. Before exiting it checks for a newer unanalysed inbound message and re-enqueues itself if there is one.

TR-AI-06

TR-AI-08
Knowledge ingestion and retrieval
  • FAQ: one chunk, Q: … \nA: …. Text: as written. URL: fetched with SSRF protection (SEC-09), main text extracted with trafilatura. File: uploaded to Cloudinary as raw, text extracted with pypdf, python-docx or as plain text.

TR-JOB-03
Postgres holds the schedule

Scheduled messages, publish targets, pending sends and metric snapshots are rows with a due time. dispatch_due claims due rows and enqueues their job with a deterministic queueing lock:

UPDATE scheduled_messages SET status = 'sending', claimed_at = now(), attempts = attempts + 1
 WHERE id IN (SELECT id FROM scheduled_messages
               WHERE status = 'scheduled' AND send_at <= now()
               ORDER BY send_at LIMIT 200 FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED)
RETURNING id;            -- enqueue(send_scheduled, key=f"smsg:{id}", id=id)

sweep_stuck returns rows left in sending or publishing for more than 10 minutes to their due state (or failed after 5 attempts), and re-enqueues webhook_events still received after 60 s. dispatch_due also pauses automations whose ends_at has passed and notifies their creator (FR-AUT-17); the runtime ignores automations outside their run window, so nothing needs to start them.

TR-JOB-04
Retries that actually retry

Each task's retry strategy (PlatformRetry, a small subclass of the queue's base retry strategy) retries only retryable PlatformErrors, with backoff 10 s × 2attempt capped at 10 minutes, or at the platform's reported regain time for rate limits, keeping the row in its in-progress state. The row is marked failed only on the final attempt or a non-retryable error. Never mark failed and then re-raise.

TR-JOB-05
Sends whose outcome is unknown

A timeout after the request was sent means the platform may have delivered it. Do not retry automatically: mark the message failed with delivery_unknown. If an Instagram echo arrives within 2 minutes with identical text in that conversation, attach its message id and mark the message sent. The user can retry manually otherwise.

TR-JOB-06
Lanes and fairness

Two lanes, each with its own worker process, so bulk work never delays a customer's DM. interactive: webhook processing, DM analysis, suggestions, automations, sends, publishing, profiles, inbound media, notifications, the dispatcher. bulk: comment analysis, summaries, knowledge ingestion, backfills, media sync, metric snapshots, digests and maintenance. At most BULK_CONCURRENCY_PER_WORKSPACE (4) bulk jobs per workspace run at once, enforced by a Valkey semaphore bulk:{workspace_id} with a 10-minute lease; a bulk job that cannot take a slot re-schedules itself 5 s later without counting as a retry. Queue depth and the age of the oldest ready job per lane are exported as metrics; alert when the interactive lane's oldest job is older than 30 s.

TR-JOB-07
Private-reply queue

A comment automation match inserts its automation_runs row with result = 'queued' and enqueues drain_private_replies(account_id) (key prq:{account_id}). The job takes as many tokens as the account's 750/hour bucket allows, sends that many queued private replies in the automation's order (oldest or newest first; public-reply-only runs skip the DM), marks runs whose comment is older than 7 days skipped_expired, and re-defers itself while runs remain. ETA = queued runs ÷ 750 per hour, returned by the automation endpoints (FR-AUT-10).

2.12Security requirements

SEC-01
No cross-workspace access

Enforced by TR-TEN-01…05. Any finding here is a release blocker.

SEC-02
No secrets in responses

Response schemas never contain tokens. A test walks the OpenAPI response schemas and fails on any property whose name contains token, secret or password.

SEC-03
Tokens encrypted at rest

Platform tokens are stored only in *_enc columns, encrypted with MultiFernet built from TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEYS (comma-separated; the first key encrypts, all decrypt). uv run python -m socialhood.security.rotate re-encrypts with the newest key.

SEC-04
Webhooks fail closed

TR-WH-02. The app refuses to start without every webhook secret it needs.

SEC-05
CORS

Allowed origins come only from CORS_ALLOWED_ORIGINS (exact matches). Credentials are not allowed because auth uses bearer tokens.

SEC-06
Secrets handling

Secrets come only from environment variables. .env* files are git-ignored and never copied into build output. CI runs gitleaks on every push.

SEC-07
Log redaction

A structlog processor removes values for keys matching token|secret|authorization|password|signature|code and truncates any text or body field to 40 characters at INFO level.

SEC-08
Input limits

API bodies ≤ 1 MB (webhook routes ≤ 5 MB, TR-WH-01); every string field has a maximum length in its schema; user content is never rendered as HTML.

SEC-09
SSRF protection

URL knowledge sources: http(s) only; resolve DNS and reject private, loopback, link-local and metadata ranges; re-check every redirect (max 3); 15 s timeout; 2 MB limit; HTML only.

SEC-10
Prompt injection containment

In R1, model output never calls tools or changes settings; it only becomes text that passes the policy (TR-AI-07) and output filter. Customer and knowledge text are passed as data, never in the system prompt.

SEC-11
Headers

API: HSTS, X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, Referrer-Policy: no-referrer, Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors 'none'. Web: a CSP allowing only the app, Clerk, Cloudinary, Meta's SDK (Embedded Signup page only), Sentry and the API.

SEC-12
Operational endpoints

/metrics requires Authorization: Bearer {METRICS_TOKEN}. /healthz returns {"status":"ok"} with no dependency details. /readyz checks Postgres and Redis and is not exposed publicly.

SEC-13
Dependencies

pip-audit and pnpm audit --prod run in CI; high or critical findings fail the build. Dependabot opens weekly updates.

SEC-14
No dev features in production

Settings validation refuses to start when APP_ENV=production and any of SANDBOX_PLATFORM_ENABLED, debug routes or relaxed verification is on.

// src/lib/api/client.ts
import createClient, { type Middleware } from "openapi-fetch";
import type { paths } from "@socialhood/api-client";

export function makeApi(getToken: () => Promise<string | null>) {
  const api = createClient<paths>({ baseUrl: process.env.NEXT_PUBLIC_API_BASE_URL });
  const auth: Middleware = {
    async onRequest({ request }) {
      const token = await getToken();
      if (token) request.headers.set("Authorization", `Bearer ${token}`);
      return request;

  };
  api.use(auth);
  return api;

3.2Sign-up and connect

F-01 Sign up and first run

  1. Visitor opens /sign-up and completes Clerk sign-up (email with verification code, or Google).
  2. Clerk redirects to /app. The resolver calls GET /v1/me; the API provisions user, workspace, owner membership and Free subscription on first sight (TR-AUTH-03).
  3. The resolver redirects to /w/{slug}/home because no account is connected.
  4. Home shows the checklist (FR-ACC-04): Connect an account · Add knowledge · Choose an AI mode · Create an automation. The first incomplete step is expanded with its button.

Edge cases. Two tabs finishing sign-up together create one workspace (unique constraint + re-read). A Clerk account with no name gets "My workspace". If /v1/me fails, show the full-page error from §4.7 with Retry; never redirect back to sign-in (v1 looped).

F-02 Returning user

  1. Signed-in user opens any app URL. Clerk middleware lets it through; a signed-out user goes to /sign-in?redirect_url=… and returns after sign-in.
  2. /app reads users.last_workspace_id from /v1/me and redirects to its inbox.
  3. Opening /w/{slug}/… for a workspace the user is not in renders "Workspace not found" with a link to /app.

F-03 Connect Instagram

  1. User clicks Connect Instagram (Settings → Connections, or the checklist). Web calls POST …/social-accounts/instagram/connect. The API checks role and accounts_per_platform, stores the OAuth state (TR-PL-08) and returns authorize_url. The browser navigates there.
  2. User approves on Instagram. Instagram redirects to GET /v1/oauth/instagram/callback?code&state.
  3. API validates and deletes the state, exchanges the code, gets the long-lived token, reads /me. It rejects account_type other than BUSINESS or MEDIA_CREATOR with ?error=ig_not_professional.
  4. If this platform_account_id is active in another workspace: redirect with ?error=account_in_use. If it exists in this workspace (reconnect): update token and status. Otherwise insert social_accounts with ai_mode from the plan (FR-SUG-01).
  5. API subscribes the account to webhooks, enqueues sync_media and backfill_account, publishes social_account.updated, and redirects to /w/{slug}/settings/connections?connected=instagram.
  6. The page shows a success toast "Instagram connected: @{username}". The checklist step ticks. Backfilled conversations appear in the inbox as they are stored.

Edge cases. User cancels on Instagram (error=access_denied): toast "Connection cancelled". Expired or reused state: ?error=state_invalid, toast "That link expired. Try connecting again." Webhook subscription fails: the account is saved with status error and last error "Couldn't subscribe to messages", with Retry on the card.

F-04 Connect WhatsApp

  1. User clicks Connect WhatsApp. The page loads Meta's JS SDK (only on this page) and calls FB.login with config_id for Embedded Signup v4.
  2. User completes Meta's dialog, choosing business, WhatsApp Business Account and number. The page receives the code from FB.login and waba_id and phone_number_id from Meta's session-info message event.
  3. Web calls POST …/social-accounts/whatsapp/embedded-signup {code, waba_id, phone_number_id}.
  4. API exchanges the code, reads the number's details, subscribes the app to the WABA, stores the account and returns it.
  5. Toast "WhatsApp connected: {display name} ({number})".

Edge cases. The dialog closes without finishing: no request, a neutral toast. The number is already connected elsewhere: 409 account_in_use. Meta refuses because Social Hood's weekly onboarding allowance is used up: "WhatsApp connections are paused for a few days. We'll email you when you can connect.", and the attempt is logged for follow-up. No history exists to backfill; the inbox fills as customers write.

F-05 Reconnect after a token failure

  1. refresh_tokens fails for an account, or any platform call returns account_needs_reconnect.
  2. The account becomes needs_reconnect; social_account.updated is published; an in-app and email notification is created.
  3. The inbox shows a banner "Reconnect @{username} to keep receiving messages" with Reconnect. Sends from that account are blocked with account_needs_reconnect; incoming webhooks are still stored.
  4. Reconnect runs F-03 and updates the same row. Status returns to active and the banner goes.

F-19 Install the app and turn on notifications

  1. After the first escalation or lead notification, and in Settings → Notifications, the app offers "Get alerts on your phone".
  2. On iPhone, if not installed, it shows the two steps to add the app to the home screen first. Otherwise it asks for notification permission, subscribes through the service worker and posts to /v1/me/push-subscriptions.
  3. The member picks which events push (Needs you, new lead, window closing, account problems) and whether to receive the weekly digest.
  4. Notifications with the push channel enqueue deliver_push; tapping one opens its link. Expired subscriptions are removed automatically.

F-07 Reply from the inbox

  1. The composer reads conversation.reply_window. open: normal. human_agent: normal, with the note "Replying with Human Agent tag. Window closes {relative time}". template_only: text box disabled, "Send a template" button. closed: disabled with the reason and when the customer last wrote.
  2. User types and presses Enter. Web creates client_id, adds an optimistic bubble (status queued), and calls POST …/conversations/{id}/messages with Idempotency-Key: client_id and {client_id, text, attachment_ids?, template?, suggestion_id?}.
  3. API checks role, account status and reply window (server-side), inserts the message (outbound, source = human, status = queued, sent_by_user_id), applies human takeover, enqueues send_message, returns 202.
  4. Worker sets sending, re-checks the window, calls the adapter. Success: sent with platform_message_id. Retryable error: stays sending and retries (TR-JOB-04). Final failure: failed with error_code and error_message.
  5. message.updated moves the bubble to Sent (or Failed with the reason from §4.7 and Retry). Later, WhatsApp statuses or Instagram messaging_seen move it to Delivered or Read.
  6. Retry calls POST …/messages/{id}/retry: allowed only for failed, re-checks the window, sets queued, enqueues again.

Attachments. The paperclip opens a file picker; each file goes through the signed upload (TR-MED-01) with a progress ring on its thumbnail; Send stays disabled until uploads finish. Text and attachments go in one request; the adapter sends them as separate platform messages in order (text last).

F-10 Schedule a message

  1. User opens the schedule popover from the composer. The picker allows times from now + 2 minutes up to the window's closes_at minus 5 minutes, in the workspace timezone, and shows "Window closes {date time}".
  2. Schedule calls POST …/conversations/{id}/scheduled-messages (Idempotency-Key) with {text, send_at}. API checks the window and pending_scheduled_messages, stores status scheduled, returns it.
  3. The Scheduled tab and a chip in the thread ("1 scheduled · Today 18:30") show it. Edit and Cancel are available until it is claimed.
  4. At send_at, dispatch_due claims it; send_scheduled re-checks the window. Open: it creates a normal outbound message (source = human, scheduled_message_id) and runs send_message; status becomes sent or failed. Closed: status expired and a notification "Scheduled message to {name} wasn't sent: the reply window closed".

F-18 Follow-up reminder before a window closes

  1. check_closing_windows runs every 15 minutes. It selects open conversations with lead_score ≥ 60, last_inbound_at between 18 and 22 hours ago, window_reminder_for not equal to last_inbound_at, and no outbound message in the last 2 hours.
  2. For each, it sets window_reminder_for = last_inbound_at, publishes conversation.updated (signal closing_soon), and creates a notification with in-app and push channels.
  3. The list shows "Closing in 3h" in warning colour and the conversation appears under the Closing soon view.
  4. Tapping the notification opens the conversation with the schedule popover ready, limited to the remaining window. A reply or a new customer message clears the signal.

3.4AI flows

F-08 Use a suggested reply

  1. After analysis with needs_reply, in Suggest or Auto mode, suggest_reply runs (TR-AI-06). The suggestion card appears above the composer with a subtle entrance.
  2. Send: web posts the message with suggestion_id and the suggestion's text. The API marks the suggestion sent.
  3. Edit: the text moves into the composer, the card collapses to a one-line "Editing suggestion" chip. Sending posts with suggestion_id; the API compares texts, stores edit_distance (normalised Levenshtein) and marks edited_sent.
  4. Regenerate: POST …/conversations/{id}/suggestions (202); the card shows a shimmer until suggestion.created replaces it. Limited to 5 regenerations per message.
  5. Dismiss: POST …/suggestions/{id}/dismiss; the card closes.
  6. Not in knowledge: when can_answer is false the card reads "Not in your knowledge: {missing_info}" with "Add to knowledge", which opens the knowledge drawer with an FAQ prefilled with the customer's question.
  7. A new inbound message supersedes the pending suggestion; a typed reply sent without using the suggestion marks it dismissed automatically.

F-09 Auto mode and human takeover

  1. Owner sets an account to Auto in Settings → Connections or from the thread header menu. The API checks ai_modes includes auto. A confirmation dialog explains what Auto does and lists the escalation rules.
  2. For each suggestion in Auto, decide_auto_reply evaluates TR-AI-07.
  3. Sent: an outbound message with source = ai_auto goes through send_message. The bubble carries the label "Sent by AI" and a small info button showing why (the decision's checks).
  4. Escalated: the conversation gets needs_human with the reason, shows the "Needs you" chip in the list and a banner in the thread ("AI didn't reply: customer is asking for a refund"), the suggestion stays for the human, and a notification is created.
  5. Skipped: no visible change beyond the suggestion.
  6. Takeover: when a human message is sent (inbox, scheduled, or an Instagram echo), set ai_paused_until = now + takeover period, insert a system message "AI paused until 16:40 because you replied" and publish conversation.updated. The thread header shows "AI paused" with Resume, which calls PATCH …/conversations/{id} {resume_ai: true}.
  7. Sending any message clears needs_human and awaiting_reply for the conversation.

F-14 Set up knowledge

  1. Knowledge page shows brand voice at the top and sources below. The first visit opens brand voice with the business description empty and a hint: "Two or three sentences about what you sell and who buys it."
  2. Add source → choose FAQ, Note, Web page or File. FAQ and Note save through POST …/knowledge-sources. Web page validates the URL. File uses the signed upload with resource_type = raw, then registers the source.
  3. The source appears with "Processing"; ingest_knowledge_source extracts, splits, embeds; status becomes Ready (with chunk and character counts) or Failed with the reason.
  4. The test box sends POST …/knowledge/test {question} and shows the drafted answer with source chips, or "Not in your knowledge".
  5. Over the knowledge_characters limit, the API returns 402 and the page shows usage with an upgrade link.

F-17 Close a knowledge gap

  1. A suggestion comes back with can_answer = false and a missing_topic. The worker upserts knowledge_gaps (TR-AI-12).
  2. The Knowledge page's "Questions the AI couldn't answer" card lists open gaps: topic, times asked, last asked, an example message. Home shows the open count; the weekly digest lists the top three.
  3. "Add answer" opens the FAQ sheet with the example question prefilled. Saving posts …/knowledge-sources with gap_id; the gap becomes answered and leaves the list.
  4. The next time a customer asks, the suggestion can answer. "Dismiss" calls …/knowledge-gaps/{id}/dismiss; a dismissed topic comes back only if asked again.

F-11 Create a keyword automation

3.6Publishing flow

F-13 Schedule and publish a post

  1. Schedule → New post, or click an empty time in the calendar (which pre-fills that time). POST …/scheduled-posts creates a draft; the composer opens at /schedule/{id}.
  2. User selects accounts, adds media (signed uploads with progress; reorder by drag; each asset validated against TR-MED-02, with the crop tool for out-of-range ratios), writes the caption (counter for characters and hashtags), optionally per-account captions, and may use AI caption, hashtags or a hashtag group, add a first comment, or add a comment automation (FR-AUT-18).
  3. The preview updates live with the first account's name and picture.
  4. Schedule: choose date and time in the workspace timezone; POST …/scheduled-posts/{id}/schedule {publish_at}. The API validates (media count and types per format, caption limits, at least one account, time ≥ now + 5 min, scheduled_posts_monthly), creates one scheduled_post_targets row per account with status pending and sets the post to scheduled. Publish now uses /publish-now, which sets publish_at = now. Add to queue calls POST …/scheduled-posts/{id}/queue, which takes each account's next free posting time and then schedules the same way.
  5. At publish_at, dispatch_due claims each target; publish_target checks the account's quota, creates containers (children first for carousels), stores container_id, and enqueues poll_container.
  6. poll_container checks status every 60 s for 5 minutes, then every 5 minutes (Meta asks for at most one status call per minute): FINISHED → media_publish, store platform_media_id and permalink, target published; ERROR or EXPIRED → target failed with the reason; still IN_PROGRESS after 30 minutes → failed "Instagram took too long to process the video".
  7. The post's status derives from its targets: all published → published; some → partially_published; none → failed. Failure creates a notification with the reason. The published post is inserted into media_items so comments and snapshots attach to it. If the post has a first comment, post_first_comment is enqueued. Automations scoped to this scheduled post, or to the account's next post, are linked to the new media item in the same transaction; a media item that first appears through sync or a comment webhook links next-post automations the same way (FR-AUT-18).

Edits. Scheduled posts can be edited or unscheduled (back to draft) until the first target is claimed; after that, the composer is read-only and says "Publishing started".

Calendar moves. Dragging a scheduled post calls POST …/scheduled-posts/{id}/reschedule {publish_at}. The card moves at once and returns with a toast if the API refuses (less than 5 minutes ahead, or publishing already started). Dropping a draft onto the calendar runs the same validation as Schedule; if it fails, the composer opens with the failing checklist items. Publishing and published posts cannot be dragged. On phones and with the keyboard, "Move to…" opens the date and time picker instead.

Conversation flags are independent booleans rather than states: status (open or archived), awaiting_reply (latest message is from the customer and needs a reply), needs_human (set by escalation, cleared by any outbound human message), ai_paused_until (timestamp or null). Webhook events: received → processing → processed | ignored | failed, with failed → processing on retry up to 5 attempts.

4.1Principles

  1. The inbox is the product. Everything else serves it. The inbox gets the most screen space, loads first and never waits on AI.
  2. AI sits beside the work. Analysis appears as chips in the list and thread; suggestions sit above the composer; decisions are explained in place. There is no separate chatbot page in R1.
  3. Mine versus theirs at a glance. One accent, used only for what the business sends and for primary actions. Customers' messages stay neutral.
  4. Say what happened. Every send shows its real status. Every AI action says why. Every limit says which limit and what to do.
  5. Calm density. Flat surfaces separated by 1 px hairlines, almost no shadows, a 16 px gutter everywhere, one type family.

5.1Conventions

  • Keys: id uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid() on every table.
  • Job queue: the queue's own tables (procrastinate_*) are created by its migrations (TR-OPS-02). They are not listed here, and the app never writes to them except through jobs.enqueue.
  • Time: created_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now() and, on mutable tables, updated_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now() maintained by SQLAlchemy onupdate. All times are UTC.
  • Tenancy: tables marked tenant have workspace_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES workspaces(id) ON DELETE CASCADE, an index on it, and inherit TenantScoped (TR-TEN-02). Not repeated in the column lists below.
  • Enums: text columns with a CHECK (col IN (…)) constraint named ck_{table}_{column}, mirrored by a Python StrEnum. No native Postgres enum types (they are hard to alter in migrations).
  • Secrets: encrypted bytea columns ending in _enc (SEC-03). They never appear in API schemas.
  • Money: integer minor units plus a currency code.
  • Naming: snake_case, plural table names, {singular}_id foreign keys, indexes ix_{table}_{cols}, uniques uq_{table}_{cols}.
  • Deletes: hard deletes with cascades. The only soft state is workspaces.status = 'deleting' before the purge.
  • Extensions (first migration): pgcrypto, vector, pg_trgm.
